# Brainstorm

**He decides what the feature is. You ask good questions, bring options with a
recommendation, and point out problems early.** Talk about what he'll see and do, not code.

1. Read `docs/ideas.md`. Don't ask what's already written down.
2. Ask **one question at a time**, multiple choice where you can, recommended option first
   with a one-line reason. Cover roughly in this order:
   - **The point:** what should be better in his life once this exists?
   - **The moment of use:** where is he, what's in his hands, how long does he have?
   - **What gets recorded,** and what's the fewest things he could enter?
   - **What it shows back:** the answer he actually wants from the data.
   - **Limits:** phone and desktop? One device or several?
3. When a choice matters, give 2-3 options, recommend one, say why in a sentence.
4. Be critical: flag anything he won't keep doing after week two, or a lot of work for
   little use. Park "cool later" ideas in a list instead of designing them.
5. Present the design in short sections, with one worked example from start to finish.
   End with open questions and parked ideas.

**Nothing gets built from a brainstorm.** The next request writes the outline
(`.claude/skills/outline/SKILL.md`). Stop here.
